Being the only child

Coderastrophy · May 2026 · CUP-26-01
ABSTRACT

The root of this traces back to my early family situation. My father passed away shortly after I was born, leaving my mother to raise me alone. Instead of remarrying, she chose to stay and support the family, even bringing in my grandmother so we could live together. The three of us grew up in the same house.

During my early childhood, my mother was constantly occupied with work moving between small businesses like shopping activities, a mini café, and later transitioning into a more stable 9/5 corporate job. Because of this, she was physically present but emotionally and temporally limited. As a result, most of my early emotional attachment and daily upbringing came from my grandmother.

My life pattern became simple: school, home, and occasional play with neighborhood friends. It was stable, but also emotionally narrow. I did not grow up with siblings, and that absence created a unique kind of emotional gap that I didn’t fully understand at the time.

As I grew older, my mother and I became closer, especially after she settled into her job. However, by that time, my emotional development had already taken shape. Soon after, I received a scholarship and moved into boarding school, which further separated me from home and familiar emotional anchors.

Over time, I began to notice patterns in myself. I became highly dependent on friendships for emotional stability, often forming strong attachments without clear boundaries. When those friendships broke or people hurt me, I experienced it very deeply and struggled to cope. I tried to fill this gap through social media and online friendships, and at one point even sought emotional validation through a romantic relationship.

After that relationship ended, I went through a depressive phase. It was also during a time when my academic performance and motivation started declining. Looking back, I realize this period was not caused by a single event, but rather a buildup of emotional instability.

Now I understand this depression as something layered: unresolved attachment issues, identity confusion, and emotional dependency patterns that developed over time. The loss of close emotional anchors first my father, then limited parental presence, then my grandmother’s passing, and later unstable relationships contributed to a fragile internal sense of security.
